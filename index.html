Project 1: M3ssag1n8 and OwlDB

By far one of the coolest, yet most tedious school projects I have worked on. M3ssag1n8 was a Slack style messaging application built in TypeScript, but what made the project especially interesting was connecting it with OwlDB, a NoSQL database my team and I built from scratch with Go. I worked across both sides of the project, from building messaging features and user interactions to working with HTTP requests, CRUD operations, JSON validation, and testing. There were definitely moments where debugging drove me crazy, but seeing two projects we built ourselves actually work together made this one of the most rewarding projects I have worked on at Rice.

Project 2: Clean Water Sweep

One of the more fun projects I have worked on because I got to take a simple idea and actually turn it into something people could play. Clean Water Sweep is a JavaScript game inspired by Minesweeper and built around charity: water's mission to bring clean water to communities around the world. Instead of looking for mines, players are trying to uncover clean water while avoiding polluted tiles. I added multiple difficulty modes, a live timer, a lives system, and real-time game state updates while also spending a lot of time trying to make the game actually look and feel good. What I liked most about this project was being able to keep changing things and seeing the game get better each time. I originally wanted to keep adding more and more features, but eventually realized that making the things that were already there better was more important. It taught me a lot about JavaScript and user experience, but more than anything, it was just really cool seeing something I came up with turn into an actual game that someone could sit down and play.

Check it out

Project 3: L'Oréal

Probably one of the more interesting projects I worked on because it felt a lot closer to something you could actually see being used. The idea was to build a skincare routine builder for L'Oréal where users could browse through real products, choose what they wanted, and build a routine around their selections. What made it especially interesting was adding an AI chatbot that could take the user's choices and help generate a more personalized routine. The part that took the most thought was figuring out how to connect everything without exposing the API key on the frontend. I ended up using a Cloudflare Worker to handle the requests on the server side, which was a pretty cool introduction to thinking about security while building something people could actually interact with. I also spent a lot of time thinking about how the whole experience should flow from choosing products to getting the final routine. It was a really fun project because it combined the parts of web development I enjoy most: making something interactive, figuring out how everything connects behind the scenes, and seeing it all come together into something that feels like a real product.
